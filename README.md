# Kestrel

A client for watching Twitch without ads, with low-latency playback, chat and your follows: for iPhone, and for Android (see [android/README.md](android/README.md)).

## Features

- **Ad-free streams through ad-free servers** (Settings → Ad blocking, on by default). Kestrel asks one of the [TTV LOL PRO v1 servers](https://wiki.cdn-perfprod.com/v1/must-read/proxies) (PerfProd / Luminous) for the stream. The server requests it from a country where Twitch doesn't run ads and hands back the playlist; the video itself still comes straight from Twitch at full quality.
  - "Automatic" tries the servers in order, starting with the last one that worked; you can also pin one server.
  - Alternatives: "My own proxy" (an HTTP proxy of yours in an ad-free country; only Twitch's playback requests go through it), or "Basic" (no server: client-side breaks are skipped, stitched-in ads are swapped for an ad-free copy of the stream or hidden).
  - Signed in and subscribed to the channel (or with Turbo), Twitch shows you no ads: Kestrel checks that first and then plays straight from Twitch, with every quality (including sub-only and signed-in 1440p) and Twitch's own low latency.
  - As a safety net the player still strips ad markers and, if an ad is stitched in anyway, switches to an ad-free copy (the TwitchAdSolutions/vaft technique). Switching back afterwards doesn't restart the player (it used to, every break); only if the player gets stuck on the switch is it started again, within a few seconds, and a stall around a switch doesn't make it keep more in hand afterwards. Prefetched segments are held back during announced breaks so an ad doesn't flash on screen.
- **Player, Twitch-style:**
  - Tap the video for controls; they fade out on their own. The bar under the picture shows how far behind live it is (⏱), viewers and how long the stream has been live, to the second. The gear holds **Quality**, **Low latency**, **Record** (in VODs: **Skip muted parts** when they have them) and **Video stats**; layout switches (theater mode, chat over video, the chat overlay) are buttons on the video itself.
  - **Gestures:** double-tap for full screen (in landscape: zoom to fill / fit); pinch to fill or fit; swipe up for full screen; pull the video down to shrink it into the **mini player** (it follows your finger); in landscape, swipe down to leave full screen and left/right to show or hide chat.
  - **Raids** (Settings → Playback → Raids): when the channel you're watching raids another, a banner shows who they're raiding with Twitch's countdown. **Follow automatically** (the default, like Twitch) takes you to the raided channel when the raid goes (**Stay** keeps you where you are, **Go now** goes straight away; in the mini player it carries on in the mini player); **Ask me** offers the raided channel with a **Watch** button instead; **Off** ignores raids. Kestrel hears about raids the way twitch.tv does, from Twitch's live-event socket (Hermes, the `raid.<channel>` topic; no sign-in needed).
  - Opening another stream stops the one before it straight away (its sound used to carry on in the background until the new player was shrunk into the mini player).
  - **Picture in Picture** (a single stream): with **PiP playback controls** on (Settings → Playback, the default) it's the regular window with play and pause, where the picture can show black for a moment on the way back (iOS hands the video back to the player, which shows nothing until the stream's next keyframe). Off, it works like multi-view's: the stream's own picture moves into the window and back, so there's no black, but the window has no buttons (pause from the lock screen, Control Center or headphones).
  - **Mini player:** keeps playing in the corner above the tab bar while you browse. Tap or drag it up to open, pause or close it with its buttons, or throw it aside to close (closing stops the stream right away).
  - Tap the streamer's picture or name under the video (or on the video in full screen and theater mode) to open their channel (the player shrinks into the mini player; a channel page that's already open is gone back to, not opened twice). The heart follows them.
  - **Lock screen and Control Center:** title, channel and picture, play/pause, and for VODs the progress bar and 10-second skips, while it plays in the background.
  - Tap anywhere outside the message box (the video, chat, the bar under the video) to put the keyboard away; shrinking the player puts it away too. The message box moves with the keyboard's own motion, and the emote picker takes the keyboard's exact place, so switching between them doesn't make the box jump. (The message box only touches the text field's settings when they actually change: re-applying them on every redraw, including each step of the keyboard's own motion, made UIKit refresh the keyboard and cut its slide.) In landscape on iOS 26 and later, iOS itself puts the keyboard away without its slide, in every app; Kestrel follows it rather than faking one. In landscape, while typing, the video shrinks into the room above the keyboard so the stream stays in view.
  - **Chat overlay:** in full screen with chat put away, the speech-bubble button shows chat over the video (read-only, text with a shadow). It goes by where the picture is: when the picture leaves a deep band of black beside it (iPhone Duo's inner display held upright, or any screen wider or taller than 16:9 by enough), chat fills that band and covers none of the video; otherwise it's a see-through box in a corner of the picture itself, a small margin from its edges, stepping clear of the controls only while they show. Drag it to another corner, drag the grip on its inner corner (shown with the controls) to resize it (remembered; Reset size and Full height when you hold it), and hold it for how see-through it is.
  - **Landscape:** the video only keeps clear of the Dynamic Island, and chat sits on the side away from it, so chat text runs to the screen edge (toggle with the chat button or a swipe, drag the line between them to resize). The player turns with the phone; the full-screen button holds landscape until you turn the phone.
  - **Rewind live streams:** when the channel records a VOD of the broadcast (and it plays for you), a seek bar runs from the start of the stream to now. Drag back to watch from any point; tap LIVE or drag to the end to return. Channels without that VOD don't get the bar. Touching the bar loads the VOD out of sight and keeps it near your finger, so letting go only has to start it; the VOD stays loaded for a few minutes after you go back to live, and going back to live reuses the stream's playlists (no new token). Going back to live, the VOD keeps playing (picture and sound) until the live stream is playing again, so nothing freezes. While you're back in time, chat shows what was said at that point (from the VOD), and live chat (including what arrived meanwhile) comes back with LIVE. Seeks land on the first keyframe at or after the spot, and the seek bar holds still until they do.
  - **VOD seek previews:** dragging the seek bar shows a thumbnail of where you'll land (Twitch's storyboards), on VODs and live rewinds; the other controls step aside while you drag.
- **VODs:**
  - Start at the beginning, or where you left off: positions are saved on the iPhone and, signed in, to your Twitch account the way Twitch's player does, so the website and apps resume at the same point. Channel video lists show how far you got.
  - **Chat replay** in time with the video.
  - **Sub-only VODs play** (the [TwitchNoSub](https://github.com/besuper/TwitchNoSub) technique): when Twitch refuses a VOD's playlist, Kestrel works out where its video files sit on Twitch's VOD servers from its storyboard address and builds the quality list itself (archives, highlights and uploads; source, transcodes and audio only; HEVC renditions detected). Downloads and rewinding a live broadcast use the same fallback. The Activity log notes when a video was unlocked this way.
  - Parts Twitch muted for copyright play (silently) instead of freezing: Twitch lists them under file names that don't exist, and Kestrel corrects them like Twitch's own player. They're marked in orange on the seek bar (and "· muted" in the seek preview); while one plays, a **Skip** button shows how long it lasts, and **Skip muted parts** in the gear menu jumps past them as playback reaches them. The same goes for a live stream's VOD while rewinding.
  - A VOD of a stream that's still live shows "RECORDING". It plays as a finished VOD of what's been recorded so far (the Apple player stalls on open-ended recordings) and loads more as you near the end. If playback ever gets stuck on a broken part, Kestrel skips ahead a few seconds.
  - One layout for both orientations, so rotating, opening chat, the keyboard and the emote picker all animate instead of rebuilding the player.
  - Picture in Picture, AirPlay, background audio, tap LIVE to jump back to the live edge.
- **Starts near the live edge:** through an ad-free server, Twitch's ad announcements don't hold back the newest segment (that's what left streams that started during one a few seconds behind until LIVE was tapped).
- **Low latency:** the [Amazon IVS Player SDK](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/ivs/latest/LowLatencyUserGuide/player-ios.html) (the engine Twitch's own apps use) in Twitch's low-latency mode. It gets close to live, and stays there, by steering the playback speed alone, never by jumping to the live edge or restarting the stream (each of which empties the buffer and sets up the next stall: on a shaky stream that became a loop of skips and reloads). It works like YouTube's and ExoPlayer's live players: the speed follows how far it is from where it should be, in proportion. A second more in hand than needed plays at 1.04× (never more than 1.06×, or 1.2× after an outage, and only once there's a clear surplus: 0.4 s more than needed), easing off as it nears the margin, so it never overshoots and runs itself dry (which used to stall it right after catching up, leaving it further behind and catching up again); less in hand plays slower in the same way (never under 0.95×). It steers by what's in hand on average rather than by each reading (video arrives in pieces, so the reading goes up and down with every piece), leaves the speed alone within a fifth of a second of the margin, and never speeds up while the reading itself is low. Right after a stream starts it catches up more keenly (1.1×, up to 1.2× for the first seconds when it joined well back), since what's over then is only how far back the player joined: it's close to live within seconds. **Starts at the live edge:** right after a stream starts, the player often begins a second or two back from the newest video it has already downloaded; once it's playing, Kestrel skips over that extra once (only over video already in hand, keeping the stream's margin, so there's no rebuffer), instead of spending up to half a minute catching up by speed. When a stream first opens, the picture and sound stay hidden behind the spinner for that moment (half a second to a second, never more than 2.5 s), so it appears already close to live instead of visibly jumping ahead. It only ever does this in the first seconds after a start (mid-stream a jump empties the buffer and sets up the next stall), and the Activity log notes each skip. Every stream starts with just half a second in hand and at most a second's margin, even on channels whose video arrived unevenly last time (they used to start up to two seconds further back, and briefly started close to live only to drift back while the rest was built up); more is only kept if the stream actually stutters this time. Reopening a stream you just closed opens it afresh (it could come back as the closed, invisible player until another stream was opened). A fresh start used to begin about five seconds behind: every stream opened its own new connections to Twitch and the ad-free server one after another (now one set is shared by every stream and opened ahead, when Kestrel comes to the front or the connection changes, along with the playlist server on the iPhone), and the first seconds' buffer reading came out far too low, so it slowed down and kept more in hand instead of catching up (now read right from the start). The Activity log notes how far from live each stream started. The margin is learned from how each stream's video actually arrives: Kestrel tracks how far delivery falls behind playback when the broadcaster's upload or the connection hiccups, keeps that much plus a little air, raises it the moment a bigger hiccup comes, and brings it back down within a minute or two once none has for a while (a hiccup counts in full for a few minutes, then fades out over ten, so the next one of the same size doesn't stall it again; a dropout on the connection longer than any margin could cover is only learned up to 1.5 s). Tuned against a simulation of Twitch's low-latency delivery: on a shaky stream it stalls and changes speed less than half as often as before, for under a tenth of a second more latency. A steady stream on good Wi-Fi sits around a second from live (0.6 s in hand); a shaky one a second or two further back, but smooth. Each channel's learned margin is remembered for twelve hours (separately for Wi-Fi and cellular, and never more than 2 s to start with), so a shaky channel starts with it instead of stalling to learn it again; others start at the least the connection allows. Kestrel also watches the connection: on cellular it keeps a little more in hand from the start (0.9 s rather than 0.6 s; more in Low Data Mode), and moving between Wi-Fi and cellular isn't counted against the stream (that stall is the handoff's). iOS doesn't let apps read Wi-Fi or cellular signal strength, so it goes by what it can measure: the connection type and how each stream's video actually arrives. When the connection can't carry the best quality (the stream kept arriving at Twitch's server, yet the player still ran dry twice within a few minutes), it steps down one quality, smoothly, and tries the better one again a few minutes later (waiting longer each time it fails); a quality picked by hand is left alone. The stats show the connection and any such limit. After a stall the player carries on where it stopped. It's only restarted when that can help: when the stream is still coming in but the player is stuck (or stopped asking for it); when the stream itself has stopped coming in, it waits and says **Waiting for the stream…** under the spinner. After a long dropout leaves it more than 20 s behind, it jumps back to live once. The playlist server is contacted while the player is still being set up, and Live asks for the stream afresh while the picture keeps playing (restarting on the same playlists kept the player from reporting its latency). Twitch's master playlist carries its clock (`SERVER-TIME`, `STREAM-TIME`), which the player sets its idea of "now" from, so Kestrel moves those on by the time since it fetched the playlist whenever it serves it (served unchanged after a while, the player reported no latency at all). Through an ad-free server, Twitch hands out playlist and video servers near *that* server (Europe), so Kestrel tries the same playlists on Twitch's server near you and uses it when it accepts them (falling back if it ever refuses or shows an ad; **Twitch servers near me** in Ad blocking turns this off). Falls back to the Apple player (AVPlayer, ~8–12 s behind) if it can't play a stream. Only in builds made with the SDK (the GitHub Actions build downloads it). VODs use the Apple player.
- **Kestrel's own player** (experimental; Settings → Playback → Player): a low-latency player written for Kestrel, aiming at the same ~1 s as Amazon's. It does what Amazon's does with the system's own building blocks: each segment, including the ones Twitch is still making (its prefetch hints), is downloaded and unpacked as it arrives (Kestrel's own MPEG-TS demuxer, tested against real Twitch segments), and its frames go straight to the system's decoder and renderers on one clock (AVSampleBufferDisplayLayer and AVSampleBufferAudioRenderer on an AVSampleBufferRenderSynchronizer). The same speed steering as the low-latency player keeps it close to live, and it opens already near the live edge. Ads are handled by the same ad-free session. It reads both containers Twitch uses, MPEG-TS and fragmented MP4 (CMAF, which Twitch is moving channels to), and H.264 and HEVC renditions (not AV1 yet). Video is decoded by the display layer (moving to Kestrel's own VideoToolbox decoding if the layer keeps failing on a stream), so it carries on in Picture in Picture and the background. **Picture in Picture** has play and pause and no moment of black on the way back (the picture never leaves the player's layer). **Quality**: the best that plays smoothly (one lower after stalls, the better one tried again later), Auto, or one picked by hand. A segment hands over to the next as soon as its frames are all in, without waiting for its download to close (that wait showed as a dip every two seconds, and raised the margin), and dips from video arriving in bursts aren't counted as hiccups. The stats show how video arrives (longest gap, segment handoff), the decoder's state and errors. Multi-view keeps the regular player. The Apple TV app uses it too (Settings → Low-latency player, on by default), falling back to the Apple player if it can't play a stream.
- **Highest quality, or a steady Auto:** the top rendition is picked and kept, unless you pick Auto (then later streams start on Auto too). Auto starts at the quality that last played smoothly, and once a quality stalls or Auto has to drop straight back down from it, Auto stays below it for a while (30 s, doubling each time it fails again, up to 8 min) before trying again, so it settles instead of bouncing. Kestrel asks Twitch for every codec the iPhone decodes in hardware, so channels using Enhanced Broadcasting list their HEVC/AV1 1440p renditions; renditions the low-latency player can't decode appear as "· Apple player". Twitch only offers 1440p to some signed-in viewers in some regions.
  - Through an ad-free server, Kestrel also checks what Twitch itself offers. If that's more (e.g. 1440p the server's anonymous copy doesn't have), it's listed in the quality menu as "· no ad-free server": picking it plays this stream straight from Twitch (Basic ad blocking, so ads are possible). While it does, the qualities the ad-free server has are marked "· ad-free": picking one (or Auto) goes back to it.
- **Sign in with Twitch** (optional) on Twitch's own login page inside the app:
  - **Chat as yourself**, with room-mode notices (slow, followers-only, sub-only); hold a message to reply to its sender. Autocorrect and spell check work in the message box.
  - **Emote picker** (smiley button): every emote you can use in this channel — the channel's sub and follower emotes you have, your other subs, Twitch's, and the channel's 7TV/BTTV/FFZ emotes — with Recent first.
  - **Autocomplete:** type `:` and a few letters for emotes, `@` for people in chat. Emotes show as pictures in the message box once typed (delete the picture to remove the emote).
  - **Following** shows your Twitch follows, live channels first (sorted by viewers) and offline ones with when they were last live.
  - **Offline channels:** **Chat** on an offline channel's page opens just its chat, the player folded into a strip at the top. A stream that ends while you watch folds the same way, chat staying put. Kestrel checks every half minute whether they're live; when they are, the strip says **Live now** with the title and a **Watch** button, and the video slides open beside chat.
  - **Follow and unfollow on Twitch** with the heart (under the video and on channel pages). Twitch only accepts follows from its own website, so, like Xtra on Android, Kestrel opens twitch.tv invisibly once in a while to get the website's token. If Twitch still refuses, the channel goes in Kestrel's own Following list instead and you're told. Signed out, the heart only uses Kestrel's list.
- **Chat:** Twitch, 7TV, BTTV and FFZ emotes (animated), badges, and recent messages when you join. New messages glide in frame by frame (busy chats and VOD replays included); only messages whose pictures arrive late are redrawn. Emotes are decoded a few at a time: an animated emote's first frame shows straight away and it starts moving once its frames are ready (whole animations decode on their own two threads, and very long ones keep every second or third frame), so a burst of emotes, or the emote picker on a channel with hundreds of animated ones, never waits on the biggest animations.
- **Lists:** live channels as compact rows like Twitch's (small picture on the left; streamer, title, category and tags on the right). Each row is one tile that opens the stream (hold it for Add to multi-view, the channel or the category; in columns on a wide screen, holding lifts just the card held). Category box art takes taps as well as its name. The system's pull to refresh. Browse switches between Live and Categories with a swipe. **Search** starts with the streamers you've looked up before (a row of pictures; **Edit**, or holding one, puts a ✕ on each so a tap takes just that one off, with **Clear all** beside it) and the most watched streams right now. Results open with an ordinary tap (the keyboard goes away with them, or when you scroll; putting it away under the tap used to shift the list mid-tap and swallow it). Typing finds channels by name, live streams by a word in their title, a tag or their category (it looks through the couple of hundred most watched streams, plus Twitch's tag search for a single word), and categories; results are fetched again when you come back, so who's live stays current. The **mini player** sits in the bottom corner, just above the tab bar when the bar runs along the bottom, and beside it when it runs down the side (iPhone Duo's inner display). It aims for where it ends up from the start: the open player hides the status bar, and while it shrinks the safe area is still the wider one for a moment, which used to send it over the side bar before it jumped back.
- **Go-live alerts** (Settings → Notifications): a notification when a channel you follow (or hearted) goes live; tap it to watch. Kestrel checks every minute while it runs. iOS pauses apps soon after you leave them, so **Keep checking in the background** (on by default) keeps Kestrel running by playing silence mixed in with your other audio, and the checks go on with the phone locked (allowed for apps you install yourself, not App Store apps; it costs some battery). Swiped away or after a restart, checks stop until Kestrel is opened; iOS's Background App Refresh is the backup. Streams that went live while Kestrel was paused don't arrive as a pile of alerts when you open it (Following shows them), and three or more found at once come as one alert.
- **Instant alerts (ntfy)** (Settings → Notifications → Instant alerts): go-live alerts within about a minute, closed or not. Kestrel puts a small relay (`kestrel-live-alerts`) in your own free Cloudflare account with an API token you give it (the "Edit Cloudflare Workers" template); every minute it asks Twitch which of your follows just went live and sends each to your private ntfy topic, which the [ntfy app](https://apps.apple.com/app/ntfy/id1625396347) delivers through Apple's push service right away. Tapping one opens the stream in Kestrel (`kestrel://live/<channel>`). Kestrel keeps the relay's channel list in step with your follows; **Turn off** removes it from Cloudflare.
- **Multi-view:** up to four live streams at once (Multi-view next to Live in Following, the grid button on the player, or **Add to multi-view** when holding any stream), laid out like the multi-stream sites: every picture as big as the space allows, side by side or stacked, 2×2, or one big with the rest in a strip (focus). Tap a picture for its sound and chat (beside or under the pictures); tap the speaker on any picture to hear it alongside the others, each at its own volume (hold a picture for Volume, Hear only this one, Make it the big one, Watch on its own, Remove); double-tap to make it the big one. Pictures are fetched no sharper than they're shown, to spare data; the quiet ones pause in the background while the one you hear keeps playing. Picture in Picture (the button on the bar, or by itself when you leave the app) shows every stream together, laid out exactly as on the multi-view screen and in a window of the same shape (so going into it and back is one smooth grow or shrink, not a 2×2 grid turning into another layout; each stream's picture is one view that moves into the window and back (right as the window opens or closes) rather than being handed between views, so nothing waits for the stream's next keyframe: no stutter going in, no black coming back; the pictures sharpen back up a moment after returning, and drop to the window's smaller size a few seconds in, not as it opens), experimentally: iOS's video-call style of PiP shows a view of the app's own rather than one player, so each stream's own low-latency picture goes in it (two stacked, three or four in a 2×2 grid, nothing drawn over them, each fetched at 360–480p while it's that small), and they keep playing, as close to live as before; multi-view shows **Playing in Picture in Picture** meanwhile. The window takes no touches (iOS gives this kind of PiP no buttons), so pick what you hear before leaving; the lock screen, Control Center and headphones show the streams you hear, and **pause** stops them all and closes the window, **play** brings the sound back (the pictures come back with the app). Whether Amazon's player keeps drawing there in the background isn't documented: if the pictures freeze or go black, **Multi-view: all streams in PiP** in Settings › Playback turns it off, and PiP shows the picked stream again (the others carry on, heard ones with their sound). Coming back, the quiet streams start again one after another instead of all at once, and stalls in the first seconds back don't count against the stream's margin, so the one you hear keeps its latency; and Kestrel no longer switches the audio off on the way back (its background keep-alive did that when no single stream was open, which silenced multi-view). The bar, names, sound buttons and the outline around the picked stream fade after a few seconds so it's just the streams (the first tap brings them back). ⌄ or a pull down on the pictures (it follows your finger, like the player) tucks multi-view away while you browse: it becomes a row of the streamers' pictures where the mini player sits, the ones you're hearing ringed with a moving sound badge; tap it to bring multi-view back, hold it to remove streams or close it (no swipes on it: they went off by accident); the rest of the app stays usable meanwhile. **Add to multi-view** while browsing collects streams in that row before any starts (▶ at its end); tap it to watch them together. Opening a single stream tucks multi-view away, stopped, until you tap the row again.
- **Liquid Glass look:** the floating glass tab bar (slide a finger along it to switch sections), with glass navigation bars and menus.
- **iPhone Duo, partly folded** (following Apple's guidelines for iPhone Duo, with the system's reserved regions for the fold): nothing sits across the fold. Folded like a book, the player puts the video, its info bar and the stream's details on one side and chat on the other; folded like a laptop, the video takes the top half and chat the bottom. Multi-view splits its streams between the two sides (in focus, the big one on one side and the rest on the other), and lists of streams in columns widen their middle gap to the fold. Full screen, typing and the mini player keep their layouts, and everything goes back as it was when it's opened flat.
- **iPhone Duo:** built with the iOS 27.1 SDK, Kestrel fills both displays and follows along when the phone is opened or closed. On the inner display it's used whichever way it's held: in landscape, chat runs down one side with the video beside it, the stream's info bar under the video and its details (whole title, viewers, uptime, category, tags) in the room below. With the details showing, the info bar is just the channel (avatar, name, follow) so nothing is said twice. The status bar is hidden there, and if a strip still runs down one side, the video takes that side and runs under it (only its controls and text keep clear) while chat takes the other side edge to edge. In portrait, chat sits under the video; while typing, the info bar steps aside and the video shrinks so the message box and some chat stay above the tall keyboard. The **Theater mode** button on the video shows just the stream (halfway down its column, with the streamer's picture and name on it) and chat in landscape. Shrinking the player keeps chat on its side (the status strip coming back no longer flips it across mid-shrink). On the outer screen, the camera sits in a corner (top right held upright; top left or bottom right held sideways), and only that corner is kept clear: the picture goes as big as it can where the camera can't reach it, against the edge away from the camera, and the top bar and the controls' row go in the room left above and below it wherever they fit at full width (with chat beside the video, both sit off the picture), otherwise over the picture's edge, so nothing is ever squeezed beside the camera; the row's numbers drop the least important first (uptime, then viewers) rather than wrapping; in portrait the info bar and chat run the whole width under the status strip's clock and signal; the video runs under the side strip in portrait, landscape chat can be narrower (drag its edge), and the **Chat over video** button lays chat over a full-width video instead. Full screen gives the video the whole display. Lists show streams as cards in two columns (an even number of columns keeps the fold between them), categories in more columns. Layouts go by the window's size, not the device, so iPad and split screen get the same treatment.
- **Record:** the gear menu's **Record** saves a live stream on the iPhone at the quality you pick (handy when a channel keeps no VOD), skipping ads, with a REC badge on the player. Recordings are under **Recordings** at the top of Following: they play in Kestrel's own player, from the start or where you left off, on the stream's own clock (the time shows when in the broadcast each moment was, not how far into the recording, and a thin line over the seek bar shows the whole broadcast with the part recorded lit up; new recordings are dated from Twitch's playlists, so a recording made later in the same broadcast carries on from where it sits), with a seek bar that runs to the newest part recorded and keeps growing while the recording is made (like a stream's VOD while it's live), **LATEST** to jump there, 10-second skips, Picture in Picture and background audio. **Download** (hold a video in a channel's Videos) saves it to watch offline: Best, 1080p, 720p, 480p, 360p or audio only, each with roughly how big it'll be (the nearest quality the video has is used), into the same list; the video shows its percentage while it downloads and **Saved** once it has, and holding it again stops the download or opens Recordings (a percentage while it downloads; it can be watched already, and stopping keeps what's saved). **Record when live** (the ⏺ button on a channel's page, or the gear menu's Record) records a channel by itself whenever it goes live, even with Kestrel closed and the phone locked: Kestrel checks scheduled channels about every minute, starts recording, and sends a notification when it starts and when it's done (tap to open Recordings; **Watch live** in a recording still being made jumps to the stream). iOS only keeps an app running in the background while it plays sound, so **Run in the background** (on by default, under Recordings) keeps Kestrel running by playing silence mixed in with other audio while channels are scheduled or recordings are going; Apple allows that only for apps you install yourself, not App Store apps, and it costs some battery. Background App Refresh is the backup if iOS stopped Kestrel anyway. Swiped away or after a restart, Kestrel can't record until it's opened again; a stretch it was paused is a jump in the recording.
- **Audio only:** playing the audio-only rendition shows a card saying so instead of a frozen picture.
- **Skip 10 seconds:** back and forward buttons beside play/pause in VODs and while rewinding a live broadcast (from live, back jumps into the recording, which runs a little behind), and on the lock screen.
- **Battery:** the mini player fetches nothing sharper than it shows (on Auto: 480p, full size again when it opens), the stream's clock under the video stops ticking while the controls are hidden, chat collects messages in the background and lays them out only when you're back, its scrolling runs at 60 frames a second rather than ProMotion's 120, and the player's twice-a-second check lets iOS fold it in with other work.
- **Video stats:** a Twitch-style overlay (✕ to close it), kept inside the picture and clear of the camera and the controls on every side, in whichever layout fits the room there is (one column, two or three, smaller type, then just the essentials), with player, quality, codecs, resolution, frame rate, bitrate, bandwidth (marked "last measured" when the player hasn't measured it lately: in low latency the video arrives as it's made, so there's rarely anything to measure), buffer, latency, playback speed (catching up or building margin) and the stream's learned margin, Twitch's video server (near you or the ad-free server's), Auto's current limit, dropped frames, ad state and ad-blocking route.

**Starting fresh:** deleting an app on iOS keeps what it saved in the Keychain, so Kestrel now clears its Keychain items (the Twitch login, your proxy password) the first time it runs after a reinstall. **Settings → Reset Kestrel** erases everything it has stored at any time, then closes it so it opens as new.

**Settings → Activity log** shows which ad-free server delivered each stream (and where Twitch thinks you are), ad breaks, player fallbacks, and image hosts that fail to load.

## Apple TV

**Kestrel for Apple TV 4K** (the `Kestrel-tv-ipa` artifact, tvOS 17+), made for the Siri Remote:

- **Live**, **Categories**, **Search** and **Following** as rows of cards (focus lifts them); Following shows your follows that are live.
- **Sign in with a code**: Kestrel shows a code and a QR code; enter the code at twitch.tv/activate on your phone or computer (or scan the QR code) and the TV is signed in. No typing a password with the remote. The login is kept in the Apple TV's Keychain and renewed when it runs out.
- **Player**: full screen, with **chat beside the video** (left or right on the remote shows or hides it), play/pause (resuming goes back to live), a click shows who and what's playing, Back closes it.
- **Ad blocking** through the same ad-free servers as the iPhone app (Settings), with ads covered and muted if one gets through anyway.
- It shares the iPhone app's core (Sources/Core, Sources/Twitch): the ad-free stream sessions, the local playlist server, the Twitch API and chat. Amazon's low-latency player isn't available for Apple TV, so streams play in the Apple player, kept about 4–5 seconds behind live (instead of its usual 8–12).

Sideload `Kestrel-tv.ipa` onto the Apple TV with a tool that supports tvOS (e.g. Sideloadly, or Xcode's Devices window with your own signing).

## Install

Download the IPA from the latest GitHub Actions run (**Actions → Build IPA and APK → Artifacts**) and sideload it with your usual tool (SideStore, AltStore, Feather, TrollStore…). Requires iOS 16+.

- **Kestrel-ipa**: the iPhone app (Xcode 27.1, iOS 27.1 SDK).
- **Kestrel-tv-ipa**: the Apple TV app (tvOS 17+). See [Apple TV](#apple-tv).
- **Kestrel-apk**: the Android app (Android 8.0+). Open the APK on the phone to install it; it can be shared with anyone as is. See [android/README.md](android/README.md).

## Build

**GitHub Actions** (no Mac needed): push to your repo. The `Build IPA and APK` workflow makes one build of each app:
- iPhone: on GitHub's Xcode 27 runner with Xcode 27.1, it downloads the Amazon IVS Player SDK (pinned version, SHA-256 checked) and runs `./build.sh` → `Kestrel-ipa`. If the IVS SDK can't be downloaded or linked, it builds without the low-latency player and flags a warning on the run.
- Android: Gradle in `android/` → `Kestrel-apk`.

**Locally** (macOS with Xcode 27.1 or later): run `./build.sh`. For the low-latency player, unpack `https://player.live-video.net/<version>/AmazonIVSPlayer.xcframework.zip` into `vendor/` (or set `IVS_XCFRAMEWORK`).

**Core tests** (playlist parsing, ad handling against a simulated Twitch and ad-free server, the local server, chat parsing): `./Tests/run.sh`. Needs a host Swift toolchain (Linux or macOS).

## Limitations

- The ad-free servers are run by third parties (PerfProd, Luminous). They see which channel you open, not your Twitch login; if none answers, Kestrel falls back to Basic ad blocking.
- Streams fetched through an ad-free server are requested anonymously, so 1440p that Twitch reserves for signed-in viewers may not be listed there (channels you're subscribed to play straight from Twitch and keep it). When Twitch offers more, the quality menu says so; or switch Ad blocking to Basic to always play straight from Twitch.
- Following on Twitch depends on Twitch's website handing out its token to Kestrel's invisible page; Twitch can change that at any time (then the heart falls back to Kestrel's own list).
- The Amazon IVS Player SDK isn't open source; it's distributed by AWS under its own terms and isn't included in this repository.
